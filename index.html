Methodology

Methodology & Limitations

Source

GDELT 2.0 Global Knowledge Graph, sampled every 4 hours (6 files/day) across three weeks chosen for thematic contrast: late October 2025 (US election cycle), late February 2026 (Milano-Cortina Winter Olympics), and mid-April 2026 (recent science/tech cycle).

Topography

The world is rendered as a single continuous heightfield mesh (~28,000 vertices). Each vertex's elevation is the sum of contributions from all reporting countries within its region of influence. A country's influence radius scales with its land area (about 4° for Israel, up to 16° for Russia), so reporting from large countries shapes a broader landscape than reporting from small ones.

Per-Vertex Elevation

For each country C and each vertex V within range, the contribution is:
log(C.count + 1) × SCALE × falloff(distance(V, C), C.radius)
where falloff is a smooth cosine bump that peaks at the country's centroid and drops to zero at its radius. Contributions are summed with a hard cap to prevent runaway peaks where many active countries overlap.

Per-Vertex Color and Texture

Each vertex's color is the contribution-weighted average tone of nearby countries. High-volume countries dominate the color near them. Negative-tone regions also receive per-vertex displacement noise — small randomized height jitter that gives them a rough, broken silhouette. Positive-tone regions stay smooth.

What This Does Well — And What It Doesn't

Well: The landscape reads as topography. Multi-country regions (e.g., Western Europe) form proper mountain ranges with ridges and valleys. The eye can pick out major reporting hubs at a glance.
Less well: Country boundaries are hinted at by the basemap below the heightfield, but elevations spill across borders. This is geographically a small lie — a country's signal influences a region beyond its actual territory. We trade strict geographic fidelity for a readable landscape.

What Tone Means — Honestly

GDELT's tone is a noisy signal. It blends sentiment of language used, the inherent valence of the topic (a hurricane scores negative even when reporting is sympathetic), and source-level editorial bias. Treat colors as directional indicators, not precise measurements.

Reporter Country Attribution

Derived from the publishing URL's TLD or, for generic .com/.org domains, a curated lookup of major outlets. About 13% of GDELT records are kept — the rest drop because we couldn't confidently country-attribute them. This biases the dataset toward English-language Western media. Russian, Chinese, and many regional outlets are systematically underrepresented.

Sports Coverage Is Thin

GDELT's theme taxonomy doesn't strongly tag sports content. Even during Olympics week, our keep rate for sports articles is low. The "limited" flag in the toggle reflects this.

Schadenfreude / Affinity Math (used in Mode II)

For each (reporter, mentioned, domain, date) bucket, we compute a delta_tone equal to the bucket's mean tone minus the global mean tone toward that mentioned country in the same domain on the same date. This isolates reporter-specific bias from the inherent valence of the underlying events.
